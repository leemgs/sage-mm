Please find enclosed our manuscript, **"SAGE-MM: Coordinating Heap
Configuration, Interop Allocation, and Page Reclamation in Memory-Constrained
Embedded .NET Firmware,"** which we submit for consideration as a regular
research article in the *Journal of Systems Architecture*.

## What the paper is about

Memory-constrained embedded consumer devices (digital TVs, set-top boxes, and
similar appliances) run managed application stacks in which a single process
co-locates a garbage-collected heap, native interoperability buffers, and a
working set of file-backed executable code pages inside one swapless memory
budget. In production firmware these three layers are almost always tuned in
isolation, which is locally reasonable but globally fragile: enlarging the
nursery lowers collection frequency but raises footprint; removing managed
allocations shifts cost to native marshalling; and reclaiming clean code pages
lowers residency but can reintroduce refault-driven interaction latency.

SAGE-MM is a **cross-layer coordination design**, not a new garbage collector.
It separates a build-time heap decision and a reviewed source-time interop
decision from a single, deliberately narrow online controller that adjusts only
one reclamation interval and one compaction gate, with dimensionless telemetry,
hysteresis, a cooldown, fail-closed handling of invalid telemetry, and an
explicit non-learning threshold fallback.

## Why it fits the Journal of Systems Architecture

The work sits squarely in the journal's scope of embedded and system-level
architecture: it concerns the interaction between a managed runtime and the
operating system's virtual-memory behavior on real ARM32/ARM64 platform
profiles, and it treats memory, latency, and reliability as a joint
system-architecture problem rather than a language-runtime detail.

## Principal contributions and measured findings

- A cross-layer action boundary that distinguishes build-time, source-time, and
  online interventions, so that only the controller adapts at runtime.
- An implementable controller with a causal (prequential) update order, bounded
  actions, and fail-closed guards.
- A measured ablation and policy comparison over the coordination ladder on two
  platform profiles, with 30 independent runs per condition and 95% percentile
  bootstrap confidence intervals. Under a favorable workload the fully
  coordinated policy lowers peak PSS by about 24% and GC tail pause by 37–38%
  at roughly 1% controller CPU, and the online controller recovers most of the
  refault cost introduced by static reclamation.
- A robustness study across favorable, neutral, and adverse workload regimes
  showing the design is fail-safe where no gain exists and only
  bounded-negative where reclamation backfires.

## Integrity and reproducibility

Every reported number is generated by a fail-closed pipeline directly from a
per-run measurement bundle; the build refuses to mark itself submission-ready
unless genuine measurements are supplied, and no estimated or predicted value is
ever substituted for missing data. We state explicitly which parts of the
protocol (per-architecture heap sweeps, isolated interop benchmarks, and
multi-hour endurance) are future work rather than reported results.
